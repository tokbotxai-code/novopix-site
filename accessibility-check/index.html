How a manual audit works

Together we choose the 3 to 5 most important journeys on your website, for example the home page, contact and a form. We test them by hand: with the keyboard only, with a screen reader, with enlarged text and on a phone, and we also check whether descriptions, links and headings make sense. At the end you receive a report with priorities and suggested fixes, and a note of what was not tested. It is not a certificate of compliance, and the result covers only the chosen journeys.

Who it is for

UK public sector bodies and other organisations, such as councils, universities, charities and small businesses, that want to know where their website stands on accessibility. We can also help you gather what you need for an accessibility statement (see our short guide).

The rules in brief

Most UK public sector websites and mobile apps must meet the Public Sector Bodies (Websites and Mobile Applications) (No. 2) Accessibility Regulations 2018. In short, they must meet the WCAG accessibility standard at level AA and publish an accessibility statement. We test against WCAG 2.2 level AA. Which version of WCAG the regulations currently require, and which bodies are exempt (for example, government guidance says nursery, primary and secondary schools are exempt apart from content needed for essential online administrative functions), is set out in the government guidance on GOV.UK. Organisations outside the regulations may still have duties under the Equality Act 2010.

Important: a report is a first check, not a certificate of compliance or legal advice. Automated tools find only part of the problems, so a report always says what was not tested. We do not publish the names of organisations we check.
