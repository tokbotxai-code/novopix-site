Accessibility statements for UK public sector websites: what to include

Public sector bodies covered by the Public Sector Bodies (Websites and Mobile Applications) (No. 2) Accessibility Regulations 2018 must publish an accessibility statement for their websites and mobile apps. This short guide summarises what a statement usually contains. It is a summary, not legal advice.

What a statement usually contains

  • the name of the organisation and of the website or app it covers,
  • the compliance status: fully, partially or not compliant with the accessibility standard,
  • the content that is not accessible and the reasons (for example, it does not meet the standard, it is outside the scope of the regulations, or meeting it would be a disproportionate burden),
  • how to ask for information in an accessible format,
  • how to report an accessibility problem, with contact details,
  • how to complain, including a link to the enforcement body: the Equality and Human Rights Commission in Great Britain, or the Equality Commission for Northern Ireland,
  • how and when the website was tested, when the statement was prepared and when it was last reviewed.

How to prepare one honestly

  1. Check the website automatically (free first check) and collect the errors.
  2. Add manual checks: keyboard use, a screen reader, PDF documents and videos.
  3. Describe the compliance status truthfully, including what you have not yet fixed.
  4. Publish the statement on the website, link to it from every page, and review it regularly.
A statement must reflect the real state of the website. An automated scan alone is not enough to say that a site is compliant. For the current requirements, the exemptions and a model statement, see the government guidance on GOV.UK: Make your website or app accessible and publish an accessibility statement and Accessibility requirements for public sector websites and apps.
